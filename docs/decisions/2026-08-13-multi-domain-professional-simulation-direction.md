# Multi-Domain Professional Simulation Direction

Date: 2026-08-13
Status: accepted

## Context

Meelni's current product and relaunch position are intentionally narrow: live
PM interview practice with realistic voice interviews, adaptive follow-ups,
PM-calibrated feedback, and evidence-based practice progress. That focus is the
right beachhead, but it is not the intended long-term category boundary.

The longer-term opportunity includes other interview-heavy and
judgment-intensive professions such as consulting, engineering, accounting and
finance, and selected healthcare or professional-admissions formats. These
domains share a need for realistic practice and trustworthy feedback, but they
do not share one generic question format or one universal scoring model.

Enterprise value may also extend beyond candidate preparation into interviewer
training and calibration, employee skill simulations, private organizational
rubrics, and cohort-level evidence. A public question bank, CLI, API, or MCP
server can distribute these capabilities, but none is sufficient as the core
product or moat.

## Decision

### Preserve the current PM beachhead

- The current customer-facing category remains **live PM interview practice**.
- The relaunch continues to serve PM candidates across Product Sense,
  Execution/Product Metrics, Strategy, and Behavioral interviews.
- This decision does not authorize a rebrand, a second public profession,
  enterprise administration, automated candidate ranking, or a launch action.
- Future direction must not be described as shipped product truth.

### Adopt a broader long-term platform thesis

Meelni's long-term direction is a domain-specialized simulation and feedback
system for high-stakes, dialogue-based professional practice.

The product should progress through three related use cases when evidence
supports them:

1. candidate interview practice;
2. interviewer training and calibration using synthetic candidates and
   organization-specific rubrics;
3. workplace skill simulations for judgment-heavy professional situations.

PM is the first vertical and proving ground for the underlying simulation,
evidence, feedback, and next-practice loop. Domain expansion should compound
those capabilities rather than replace them with a generic chatbot.

### Expand through complete domain packs

Every future domain must define a coherent domain pack rather than only change
the agent prompt. A domain pack includes:

- a competency and level model;
- scenario and question formats;
- interviewer behavior and follow-up policy;
- acceptable evidence types;
- evaluation modes and behavioral anchors;
- next-practice progression;
- required domain tools such as exhibits, calculators, whiteboards, code
  environments, terminals, or spreadsheets;
- provenance, versioning, and subject-matter-expert validation.

Evaluation contracts may differ by domain. They may produce deterministic
results, rubric results, behavioral-anchor results, execution results, or
scoreless coaching when evidence is insufficient. They must not flatten every
professional question into a purported single correct answer.

### Sequence depth before breadth

- PM remains the beachhead until its acquisition, completion, feedback quality,
  repeat-practice, and monetization signals are understood.
- Consulting cases are the leading second consumer-domain hypothesis because
  they reuse adaptive spoken reasoning while adding exhibits and quantitative
  checkpoints.
- Engineering expansion should begin with system design, technical leadership,
  incident response, architecture trade-offs, and AI-era judgment before
  commodity coding exercises. Coding requires a separately validated execution
  environment and evaluation contract.
- Accounting and finance are leading enterprise-learning hypotheses because
  technical judgment, ethics, client communication, and professional
  competency frameworks support employer-sponsored simulations. They require
  standards-aware domain expertise and spreadsheet evidence.
- Medical or other regulated professional simulations require a higher trust,
  validation, accessibility, and claims bar and should follow later evidence.
- The exact second domain remains an evidence-based checkpoint rather than a
  permanently fixed roadmap commitment.

### Use enterprise as a land-and-expand path

The preferred initial enterprise wedge is interviewer practice and calibration
for one judgment-heavy role, beginning with PM where Meelni has domain depth.
Expansion may then cover adjacent role families and workplace simulations.

Early enterprise work should:

- train and evaluate interviewers or learners against synthetic scenarios;
- keep consumer practice data unavailable to employers;
- keep private enterprise questions and rubrics tenant-controlled;
- avoid automated ranking of real applicants until independent validation,
  legal review, and an explicit product decision authorizes that use;
- require a real buyer signal before building broad administration or
  integration surface area.

### Treat CLI, API, and MCP as delivery adapters

- A generic terminal interview experience is not a standalone strategic wedge.
- A terminal may become useful for engineering or agentic-work simulations when
  it carries domain-relevant execution evidence.
- APIs and MCP may expose domain-aware scenario, competency, practice,
  feedback, and next-action capabilities to approved clients.
- Public company-question retrieval is an acquisition surface, not the moat.
- Private organizational questions, rubrics, and answer anchors must not be
  exposed through public tools.

## Expansion Gates

A second domain or enterprise product should proceed only when the proposed
slice has:

- a clear candidate, institutional, or enterprise buyer and struggling moment;
- qualified subject-matter experts who agree with the feedback contract;
- realistic scenarios with lawful, attributable, or licensed provenance;
- substantial reuse of the session, transcript, evidence, feedback, and
  next-practice system;
- an explicit model for any new evidence modality or tool;
- bounded claims, privacy, accessibility, and regulatory handling;
- a measurable acquisition, activation, retention, monetization, enterprise
  validation, or product-quality lever;
- evidence of willingness to practice, distribute, or pay.

Do not create premature horizontal abstractions only for hypothetical future
domains. Generalize product and code boundaries when a real second domain or
enterprise design partner exposes the reusable contract.

## Consequences

- Current PM positioning and relaunch scope remain stable.
- Product strategy should distinguish the PM beachhead from the long-term
  multi-domain platform thesis.
- Durable value should accumulate in domain competencies, adaptive simulation
  behavior, evidence contracts, evaluator calibration, progress signals, and
  trusted organizational configuration rather than in question volume alone.
- Enterprise discovery can run alongside consumer learning, but enterprise
  complexity must follow buyer evidence.
- Future domain work requires its own product decision or handoff brief before
  implementation.

## Open Questions

- What measured PM signals should unlock the first domain expansion experiment?
- Which distribution or paid-design-partner signal should choose the second
  domain?
- When should Meelni adopt a broader public category or brand architecture?
- Which enterprise use case can demonstrate repeatable willingness to pay
  without evaluating real applicants?
- Which domain-specific tools should become shared platform capabilities after
  they have been validated in more than one vertical?

## Validation

- Keep active customer-facing copy aligned with live PM interview practice.
- Test future domains through bounded prototypes and expert-reviewed fixtures
  before public launch.
- Measure expert agreement, user-rated feedback usefulness, completed practice,
  repeat practice, and paid or distribution intent for each domain.
- Record a separate accepted decision before changing the public beachhead,
  enabling real-candidate assessment, or introducing cross-domain enterprise
  administration.
